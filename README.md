AI-powered ESG &amp; carbon accounting copilot that extracts activity data, classifies Scope 1/2/3 emissions, matches verified emission factors, performs deterministic CO₂e calculations, detects compliance risks, and generates traceable audit records.
# 🌱 CarbonTrace AI

> **From business documents to verifiable carbon insights.**

CarbonTrace AI is an AI-powered **ESG & Carbon Accounting Compliance Copilot** that helps organizations transform business documents and activity data into structured, traceable, and auditable carbon-emission insights.

The system combines **Lyzr-powered AI agents** with **deterministic calculation tools** to extract activity data, classify **Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3 emissions**, match emission factors, calculate CO₂e, detect compliance risks, and generate an evidence-backed audit trail.

---

## 🎯 Problem Statement

Organizations maintain sustainability-related information across multiple sources, including:

- Electricity bills
- Fuel invoices
- Travel records
- Freight records
- Supplier documents
- ERP exports
- CSV files
- Excel files
- PDF documents

Converting this information into reliable carbon-accounting data manually is:

- Time-consuming
- Error-prone
- Difficult to audit
- Difficult to trace back to source documents
- Difficult to scale
- Vulnerable to inconsistent calculations

Using an LLM alone introduces additional risks because language models may:

- Hallucinate emission factors
- Misclassify emission scopes
- Make arithmetic mistakes
- Produce results without sufficient evidence

CarbonTrace AI addresses these challenges by separating **AI reasoning** from **deterministic computation**.

## 2. 🌍 Automated Scope 1 / 2 / 3 Classification

CarbonTrace AI categorizes activities according to their emission scope.

### Scope 1 — Direct Emissions

Direct greenhouse-gas emissions from sources owned or controlled by the organization.

Examples:

- Company-owned vehicles
- Company-controlled generators
- On-site boilers
- Fuel combustion

Example:

```text
Company-owned truck
        ↓
Diesel combustion
        ↓
Scope 1
```

---

### Scope 2 — Purchased Energy

Indirect emissions associated with purchased or acquired energy.

Examples:

- Purchased electricity
- Purchased heating
- Purchased cooling

Example:

```text
Power Grid
    ↓
Electricity Purchased
    ↓
Company
    ↓
Scope 2
```

---

### Scope 3 — Value Chain Emissions

Other indirect emissions occurring across the organization's value chain.

Examples:

- Business travel
- Third-party transportation
- Purchased goods
- Employee commuting
- Supplier-related activities

Example:

```text
Third-Party Logistics
        ↓
Transportation
        ↓
Scope 3
```

# 3. 🧮 Deterministic CO₂e Calculation

A core principle of CarbonTrace AI is:

> **LLM for understanding. Code for calculation.**

The system does not rely on an LLM to perform critical carbon arithmetic.

The fundamental calculation is:

```text
CO₂e = Activity Data × Emission Factor
```

### Example

```text
Activity:
300 litres diesel

Emission Factor:
2.5 kg CO₂e/litre

Calculation:

300 × 2.5

= 750 kg CO₂e
```

The calculation is performed by a deterministic backend service.

---

# 4. 📚 Emission Factor Matching

CarbonTrace AI uses a controlled emission-factor dataset.

Example:

| Activity | Unit | Factor |
|----------|------|--------|
| Diesel | litre | Configured factor |
| Electricity | kWh | Configured factor |
| Freight | tonne-km | Configured factor |
| Business Travel | passenger-km | Configured factor |

The factor matching process can consider:

- Activity type
- Unit
- Geography
- Reporting year
- Factor version
- Factor source

Emission factors are treated as **data** rather than being generated by the LLM.

---

# 5. 🔍 Auditability & Traceability

Every important carbon calculation should be traceable back to its source.

Example:

```text
750 kg CO₂e
      ↓
300 litres diesel
      ↓
Emission Factor
      ↓
Factor Source
      ↓
Original Document
```

An audit record can contain:

```json
{
  "source_document": "diesel_invoice.csv",
  "activity": "diesel",
  "quantity": 300,
  "unit": "litre",
  "scope": 1,
  "emission_factor": 2.5,
  "factor_unit": "kg CO2e/litre",
  "formula": "300 × 2.5",
  "result_kg_co2e": 750
}
```

This allows users and auditors to understand where each important number originated.

# 6. 🛡️ Compliance & Greenwashing Detection

The compliance layer identifies potential data-quality and sustainability-reporting issues.

Checks may include:

- Missing information
- Missing emission factors
- Unsupported environmental claims
- Suspicious activity values
- Inconsistent Scope classification
- Missing source evidence
- Potential greenwashing indicators
- Incomplete records

AI-generated recommendations are validated by deterministic rules wherever possible.

```text

                 │
       ┌─────────┴─────────┐
       │                   │

       │                   │
       └─────────┬─────────┘
                 ↓
       Deterministic Layer
                 │

    Matching
       │         │         │

                 ↓
```

### AI is responsible for:

- Document understanding

- Unit validation

- Emission-factor matching

- Data validation
- Audit records
- Calculation reproducibility

- Validate uploaded files

# 🏆 Design Principles

CarbonTrace AI is built around five principles:

### 1. AI for Understanding

Use AI where contextual understanding is valuable.

### 2. Code for Calculation

Use deterministic code for important arithmetic.

### 3. Evidence Before Claims

Important results should have supporting evidence.

### 4. Traceability by Design

Users should be able to understand where results came from.

### 5. Human-Understandable Results

Complex AI processing should result in clear, explainable outputs.
